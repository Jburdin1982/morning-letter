Tuesday opens cold, about 40 degrees at dawn under a lid of cloud, then climbs to 66 by noon and about 69 by mid-afternoon, with no rain in sight. The evening cools back into the 50s, with some clearing late.

Wednesday, Veterans Day, is the warmest day of the stretch, near 75 with a low of about 49 and a slim 7 percent chance of rain. A cold front then drops Thursday to the low 60s and Friday to the low 50s, and Saturday morning, opening day of modern gun deer season, will start near 33 degrees.

The moon is a hairline crescent, barely a day old, and will slip below the western horizon soon after the sun, so tonight is still very dark. Tomorrow is Martinmas, St. Martin's Day, and the old folk belief in a mild spell around it, a “Martinmas summer,” looks well matched by Wednesday's forecast. It is lore, not a forecast. The United States Marine Corps, for its part, marks its birthday today; it was founded on November 10, 1775.

In the garden, rake the leaves onto the beds rather than the curb: oak and sweetgum leaves make good mulch for garlic and strawberries, and a thick layer keeps the soil from heaving in a hard freeze. Collards and mustard greens are at their sweetest now, and a warm spell like Wednesday's is a fine day to clean and oil the pruning tools.

Out on the Delta the combines are mostly parked, and the winter wheat that went in after soybeans is just showing its green rows. Overhead, long skeins of geese and the first sandhill cranes are heading south; listen for the cranes' rattling call on a still evening. Hickory nuts and black walnuts are down in the woods, and the deer tracks in the soft ground of field edges are thickening, a sign of the rut.

Of Local Interest

Craighead County’s 2027 budget gets a public airing

The Craighead County Quorum Court, the county's legislative body, presented its proposed 2027 budget to the public on Monday at the courthouse annex. The plan calls for an overall increase of 1.4 percent, a 4 percent raise for county employees and a new public information officer. County Judge Marvin Day, the county's chief executive, said the new position would mainly handle Freedom of Information Act requests, the public-records requests that citizens and reporters file, and would also help with press releases and social media.

The budget may still be amended before the Quorum Court votes on it in December. Judge Day also introduced the newly elected justices of the peace, who take office in January, and said he has given each of them the current and proposed budgets so they can “hit the ground running.” (KAIT's report gives no dollar totals.)

Lake Poinsett cleared of its algae advisory

The Arkansas Division of Environmental Quality, part of the state Department of Energy and Environment, has lifted the harmful algal advisory for Lake Poinsett in Poinsett County. The advisory was issued in September after the agency confirmed a bloom of cyanobacteria, sometimes called blue-green algae, which can produce toxins harmful to people and pets. After water samples taken over two weeks, the agency said the toxin levels had “fully dissipated” and are within safe recreational levels.

The agency still asks anyone who sees a suspected new bloom to stay out of the water and report it, at 501-682-0744 or ee.habs@arkansas.gov.

Probation in the school bus threat case

A 30-year-old Jonesboro man pleaded guilty Monday to first-degree terroristic threatening, a Class D felony, over an incident on September 15 at Brazos and Kingsbury streets, and Circuit Judge Scott Ellington sentenced him to 60 months of probation and 120 days in the Craighead County jail, with credit for 55 days already served. A misdemeanor trespassing charge was dropped. According to the arrest affidavit, he went to a school bus looking for someone who had cursed at his children and, when the driver kept him from boarding, threatened the driver; bus video recorded him saying he would come back with a gun. He told police he was angry and trying to protect his children.

He must stay away from the victim, complete anger-management and parenting classes and pay $640 in fines and fees. Jonesboro Public Schools has since updated its bus rules: parents who need to speak with a driver must now do so through the driver's window, not at the bus door.

Former DHS caseworker’s trial pushed to March

The jury trial of a former Craighead County caseworker for the Arkansas Department of Human Services, who is accused of possessing child sexual abuse material, has been moved from December to March 1–12, 2027, according to Jonesboro Right Now. He was arrested in September after two tips from the National Center for Missing and Exploited Children flagged a Google account that allegedly uploaded 44 videos. He remains in the Craighead County jail on a $250,000 bond. These are allegations; he has not been convicted.

The state department says its review of every case he handled found no connection between his official duties and the material, and that it interviewed every child still in foster care in those cases. A DHS spokesman said the agency hopes to finish its internal review soon.

Child battering arrest on Melrose Street

A 20-year-old Jonesboro man faces two counts of second-degree domestic battering, both Class B felonies, after police went to an apartment on Melrose Street on Sunday, November 8. A caller had reported hearing a child being struck repeatedly for about 20 minutes. According to the probable cause affidavit, officers saw injuries on the side and stomach of a 9-year-old and a loop-shaped welt on a 4-year-old; the older child said he was hit with an extension cord, and the man at first blamed a dog.

At his first appearance Monday, District Judge Tommy Fowler set a secured bond (the amount was not reported), ordered no contact and appointed a public defender. He is due in Craighead County Circuit Court at 8:30 a.m. on December 16. The charges are allegations.

Public asked to weigh in on a Delta “heritage area”

Public comment is open until December 11 on a study to create the Arkansas Delta National Heritage Area, a congressionally designated region whose historic, natural and cultural resources are managed by local partners. The proposal covers 17 eastern Arkansas counties, Craighead among them. If Congress approves, the area would receive federal funds for 15 years; backers describe it as an economic development and tourism tool, with ideas such as music tourism and a competitive grant program for preservation and the arts.

The effort is led by Arkansas State University's Heritage Sites under Dr. Adam Long, and Sen. Tom Cotton and Rep. Bruce Westerman plan to introduce legislation. The full study is on the A-State website; comments and letters of support go by email to Dr. Long at adamlong@AState.edu.

From the State

What the 2026 campaigns cost

Arkansas's two statewide races together drew more than $25 million in reported spending, according to a Jonesboro Right Now analysis of campaign-finance data. Gov. Sarah Huckabee Sanders, who won a second term with about 54 percent, spent roughly $5.7 million by one tracker's count, about $12 for each vote she received; another tally, drawn from state ethics filings, puts her at about $9.9 million. Her Democratic opponent, state Sen. Fred Love, reported about $206,000 to $296,000, depending on the source.

In the U.S. Senate race, Republican Sen. Tom Cotton, also at about 54 percent, reported $15.8 million through October 14 against $3.3 million for Democrat Hallie Shoffner. The article cautions that the figures are preliminary and leave out outside groups' spending, which in the Senate race ran to millions of dollars.

Democrats say a smaller majority means more leverage on the budget

With Republicans holding a 70-30 House majority after Tuesday's election, down from a supermajority, most appropriations bills, which need three-fourths of both chambers, will now require some Democratic votes, House Minority Leader Andrew Collins told the Arkansas Times. He said Democrats will not automatically block spending bills but will fight policies they oppose. Governor Sanders's office said she will keep her focus on education, public safety and tax cuts and work with both parties where priorities align. The Governor's $6.97 billion budget plan now goes to the legislature, which meets in January.

From the Nation

Congress returns; the Hegseth impeachment vote looms

Congress came back Monday for a lame-duck session, the stretch between the November election and the swearing-in of the new members in January. Democrats won both chambers and will take control next year. At the top of the to-do list is a government funding bill to head off a shutdown in December; the debt limit is also on the agenda, according to CNBC.

Kentucky Republican Rep. Thomas Massie reintroduced articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth on Monday, which under House rules requires leaders to act within two legislative days, so a vote is expected by week's end. Massie's earlier resolution lapsed in September when the House left for a recess. Republicans hold a narrow 218-214 majority, and Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries has called himself a “hard yes.” Impeachment needs only a simple House majority; conviction would take two-thirds of the Senate. Hegseth has dismissed the effort.

Trump: Iran standoff stays at the “status quo”

President Trump told reporters at an impromptu White House briefing that the United States will hold the war with Iran, which began with American and Israeli strikes in February, at the “status quo” while talks continue, though he warned that American forces are “locked and loaded.” Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Sunday that talks are not on the verge of a breakthrough because Iran's demands are unacceptable. Iran says it is still talking through mediators in Qatar and Pakistan, and Qatar's prime minister was due at the White House to discuss a possible deal on the Strait of Hormuz, the shipping lane through which a large share of the world's oil passes. Oil prices fell sharply on the news (see Markets).

From Abroad

G7 ministers press Rubio on Iran and Ukraine

Foreign ministers of the Group of Seven, the club of leading industrial democracies, opened a two-day meeting in Paris on Monday. It is Rubio's first major trip overseas since the U.S. elections. European ministers want assurances that any settlement of Russia's war in Ukraine is fair to Kyiv, and Ukraine's foreign minister, Andriy Sybiha, joined the Ukraine session today. Germany's foreign minister, Johann Wadephul, said the talks would also look at ways to avoid military escalation with Iran.

Separately, U.S.-mediated talks involving Ukraine and Russia are scheduled in Abu Dhabi, in the United Arab Emirates, for Tuesday and Wednesday. The United States takes over the G7 presidency from France in 2027.

Afield & Afloat

Ratings are rule-of-thumb estimates from the forecast, the rut calendar and the sun, not measurements of animal or fish behavior.

Deer outlook: Good

The rut is at its peak, dawn is a crisp 40 degrees and the wind is nearly calm, with a slow fall in pressure ahead of Wednesday's warmth, all in the hunter's favor; the warm afternoon near 69 is the only drawback. Be settled by first light at 6:06 a.m. and hunt until about 8:30, then sit again from about 3:00 p.m. to sunset at 4:58. In the peak of the rut, 10 a.m. to 2 p.m. is realistic too, with bucks cruising.

Today's tip: bowhunters should get to the stand before the first light and stay quiet; with air this still, your scent will pool and drift unpredictably, so keep a cover scent bag or pick a stand with a creek or thicket behind you. Remember that only archery is open this week; modern gun opens Saturday.

Tomorrow, Wednesday (estimate: Good): dawn near 49, a warm afternoon near 75 and a southerly wind rising to about 13 mph by evening. Watch about 6:05 to 8:35 a.m. and 2:57 to 4:57 p.m. Tomorrow's tip: the warm afternoon will push deer into shade, so favor the morning sit and the thick bedding cover on the downwind side of a field.

Fishing outlook: Good

At the Crowley's Ridge lakes (Craighead Forest Lake, Lake Frierson), steady pressure and a mild afternoon make a good day: fish late morning through mid-afternoon as the shallows warm, then again in the last hour before sunset at 4:58.

Today's tip: crappie are moving to deeper brush and docks as the water cools; try small minnows or 1/16-ounce jigs under a float at 8 to 12 feet, and slow-roll a spinnerbait along wood for bass.

Tomorrow, Wednesday (estimate: Fair): pressure drops fast ahead of the cold front, which fish tend not to like. Late morning to mid-afternoon is best, with a second window before sunset at 4:57. Tomorrow's tip: fish slowly and a bit deeper, and expect wind to stir the surface by afternoon.
