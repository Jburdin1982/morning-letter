At the eleventh hour of the eleventh day of the eleventh month of 1918, the guns of the Great War fell silent under the Armistice signed in a railway carriage in France. The day was kept here as Armistice Day until 1954, when Congress renamed it Veterans Day to honor all who have served. A minute of quiet at 11 o’clock this morning is an old custom worth keeping.

The moon is a slim two-day crescent; look low in the southwest in the half hour after sunset, before it follows the sun down. It reaches first quarter around November 17 and is full around November 24.

Enjoy today’s warmth, because Saturday’s dawn near 34 is close to frost in the low spots. Bring in potted herbs and geraniums, drain and coil the garden hoses, and pick the last green tomatoes to ripen on a windowsill. Turnips and collards will only sweeten with the cold.

In the woods, wild persimmons are softening on the bare twigs; they are best after a frost has wrinkled them, and the deer and possums know it. Old-timers split a persimmon seed to read the winter: a spoon shape means snow to shovel, a knife means cutting cold, a fork means mild. It is lore, not a forecast. At the feeders, watch for the dark-eyed juncos, the slate-gray “snowbirds” that arrive from the north about now and stay until spring.

Prosecutors drop child-rape case for lack of a witness

Prosecutors have dismissed the charges against a Jonesboro man arrested in March 2025 and accused of raping a 10-year-old girl. Circuit Judge Scott Ellington signed an order of nolle prosequi, the formal notice that the state will not go forward with the case, on Monday, November 9. The dismissed charges were rape, possession of firearms by certain persons and aggravated assault.

In the filing, Deputy Prosecuting Attorney Alexia Perkins wrote that the victim was “either unavailable or uncooperative.” The judge added a handwritten note that the child was not available to testify and that her parent would not make her available for trial. The man, who had been held on a $250,000 bond, has been ordered released. The reports did not say whether the charges could be filed again; he was never convicted.

Brookland restaurant manager charged with draining a teen’s pay card

A manager of the McDonald’s in Brookland faces two felonies and a misdemeanor after Brookland police say she cloned a teenage employee’s payroll debit card. A Craighead County district judge found probable cause on Monday for financial identity fraud, a Class C felony; theft of property involving credit or debit cards, a Class D felony; and fraudulent use of a credit card or account number, a misdemeanor. Bond was set at $35,000 and her next court date is December 16.

According to police, managers have access to a safe of spare employee pay cards and can link a card to an employee’s account. Investigators allege she linked an unissued card to the girl’s account; on August 12, while the employee was in Memphis, her paycheck was drained in about half an hour through two ATM withdrawals totaling $760 at a Paragould bank. Police say bank surveillance photographs identified the manager. These are allegations; she has not been convicted.

Prison term for spitting on hospital staff

A 29-year-old man from Alicia, in Lawrence County, was sentenced Monday to 18 months in prison, with four more years suspended, after pleading guilty to felony assault charges for attacks on medical workers in two Jonesboro emergency rooms. In the early hours of August 2 at St. Bernards Medical Center he spat on a doctor, two nurses and a county jailer, tried to bite staff and threatened to “shoot up the whole hospital”; in July he had spat in a doctor’s face at NEA Baptist.

Arkansas law makes aggravated assault on a health-care worker a felony. Circuit Judge Scott Ellington ran the sentences together, credited 101 days already served, ordered no contact with the victims and set monthly fines after release. A terroristic-threatening charge and a disorderly-conduct charge were dropped in the plea.

A Beebe gift for the Bradbury

Former Gov. Mike Beebe, a Searcy native who led Arkansas from 2007 to 2015, has given a painting called “Beau” to the permanent collection of the Bradbury Art Museum at Arkansas State University. From the State

Justice Hiland replaces Judge Rudofsky as appeals-court nominee

The White House on Tuesday withdrew its nomination of U.S. District Judge Lee Rudofsky, a federal trial judge in Little Rock and former Arkansas solicitor general, to the 8th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals, and President Trump named Arkansas Supreme Court Justice Cody Hiland instead. The 8th Circuit is the federal appeals court just below the U.S. Supreme Court for Arkansas, Missouri, Iowa, Minnesota, Nebraska and the Dakotas; the seat opens because Judge Bobby Shepherd of Arkansas is taking senior status, a form of semi-retirement.

Hiland was U.S. attorney for the Eastern District of Arkansas from 2017 to 2020, and his state court term ends December 31. Arkansas’s Republican senators, John Boozman and Tom Cotton, had never publicly backed Rudofsky, and outlets report Hiland is their choice. Rudofsky said that without his home-state senators, “confirmation is simply not a practical possibility,” and called Hiland “a solid jurist and a good friend.”

Time is short: the Senate must confirm him by January 3, when the new Democratic majority takes over, or the nomination lapses. Judiciary Chairman Chuck Grassley says he will push judges through in the lame-duck session.

Medicaid expansion overhaul will take longer than Washington allowed

ARHOME, the Arkansas Medicaid expansion program that uses federal money to buy private insurance for about 208,800 low-income working-age adults, has a federal extension only through June 30, 2027; the state had asked for two years. On Tuesday, Elizabeth Pitman, who directs the Division of Medical Services at the state Department of Human Services, told legislators that moving those enrollees to traditional fee-for-service Medicaid would take 12 to 18 months, and a move to managed care at least two years. She said federal officials signaled more extensions “as needed.” “Nothing is off the table except for not continuing coverage,” she said.

Meanwhile, about 70,000 enrollees in Centene plans must choose a Blue Cross plan by November 15 or be assigned one, because Centene is leaving the program, and the full Medicaid work requirement of 80 hours a month of work, school or volunteering takes effect January 1. Lawmakers say a new expansion model will be a top priority when the session opens in January.

A new idea for the stalled prison plan

At a joint meeting of the legislature’s Judiciary committees on Tuesday, state Rep. Steve Unger, a Springdale Republican, proposed building two 500-bed state prisons beside the Washington County jail in Fayetteville, using money set aside for Gov. Sanders’s planned 3,000-bed prison in Franklin County. That project’s estimated cost had grown from $470 million to $825 million amid local opposition and water-supply problems, and corrections officials shelved it in April. No action was taken. Sen. Clarke Tucker, a Little Rock Democrat, noted the prison population is about 19,000, below the 21,000 once projected, and is pushing proposals to reduce the number of beds needed.

From the Nation

House shelves the Hegseth impeachment, 211–204

The House voted 211 to 204 on Tuesday to send Rep. Thomas Massie’s articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to the Judiciary Committee, which effectively ends them. Massie, a Kentucky Republican who leaves Congress in January after losing his primary, was the only Republican to vote with all Democrats against the move. His articles accused Hegseth of starting the war with Iran without approval from Congress, of strikes on boats in the Caribbean that critics call unlawful killings, of directing the seizure of Venezuela’s president, Nicolás Maduro, and of retaliating against Sen. Mark Kelly.

Hegseth called the effort a “farce,” and President Trump said it “miserably FAILED!” House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries said, “This is not the end of it.” Massie says he will not file again but will force a vote on a war-powers resolution on Iran; Democrats could take up impeachment once they control both chambers in January.

Republicans pick their leaders for the minority

After losing both chambers in last week’s election, House Republicans on Tuesday chose Rep. Jim Jordan of Ohio, chairman of the Judiciary Committee and a close Trump ally, as their leader in the next Congress, by 106 to 73 over Majority Leader Steve Scalise of Louisiana. Speaker Mike Johnson had already said he would not seek the post. Rep. Tom Emmer of Minnesota stays as whip and Rep. Lisa McClain of Michigan becomes conference chair.

Senate Republicans re-elected John Thune of South Dakota as their leader without opposition. Arkansas’s Sen. Tom Cotton keeps the No. 3 job, chairman of the Senate Republican Conference. Dozens of Republican National Committee members had asked for a 30-day delay to study the losses; the votes went ahead. ABC counts Democrats ahead in the House, 254 to 166, with 15 races still undecided.

Prices rose faster than expected in October

The Bureau of Labor Statistics, the Labor Department agency that tracks prices, said consumer prices rose 0.4 percent in October, after 0.6 percent in September. Core prices, which leave out food and energy, rose 0.3 percent, more than economists expected. Energy rose 0.7 percent, food 0.5 percent and beef 0.9 percent; housing costs made up nearly a third of the increase, while used cars fell. The bureau could not publish a yearly rate because the 2025 government shutdown kept it from collecting prices in October 2025.

Traders now expect the Federal Reserve, the nation’s central bank, to raise interest rates by a quarter point at its December 8–9 meeting; the benchmark rate is now 3.75 to 4 percent. Fed officials cite tariffs, the Iran war and spending on artificial-intelligence data centers among the pressures on prices.

Senate Democrats divided over Schumer

Sen. Chuck Schumer of New York, the Democratic leader, said Tuesday he is “confident I will be elected majority leader” when Democrats take control in January, but opposition is growing. Sen. Bernie Sanders of Vermont said, “I will not be supporting Chuck,” and Sen. Elissa Slotkin of Michigan said she is a “no” until someone offers a concrete plan for change; Fox News reports five newly elected Democratic senators also oppose him. No challenger has declared. The secret-ballot vote, which includes the senators-elect, is expected in early December.

From Abroad

Iran: Rubio says the blockade is working; Tehran says its uranium stays

Secretary of State Marco Rubio said in Paris on Tuesday, after the meetings of the G7 industrial democracies, that President Trump “has concluded that for the time being there is no need for major combat operations” against Iran, because sanctions and the U.S. naval blockade are “achieving the same objective by economic means.” He called Tehran’s conditions for talks “absurd,” though he offered no figures for his claim that Iran cannot sell its oil.

On Wednesday, Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran’s Supreme National Security Council, its top security body, told Lebanese television that Iran’s enriched uranium will never leave the country and that no talks are under way; he warned of strikes on U.S. bases if Iran is attacked again. Attacks on shipping in the Strait of Hormuz have not eased, Reuters reports, and Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said two tankers caught fire after hitting mines. Brent crude rose about 1.4 percent early Wednesday.

Ukraine talks in Abu Dhabi end without a breakthrough

The second day of American-brokered talks between Russian and Ukrainian officials in Abu Dhabi, capital of the United Arab Emirates, ended Wednesday after less than two hours, with no word of progress on the main disputes: territory, security guarantees and the terms of a ceasefire. It was the first face-to-face meeting of the two sides since February. Russian state media said both sides agreed to another large prisoner exchange; Kyiv had not confirmed it.

The fighting went on. Russia struck six Ukrainian power facilities in four regions, leaving parts of the city of Kryvyi Rih without power or heat, and Ukrainian drones set a fire at Russia’s Ust-Luga port on the Baltic, injuring three workers.

U.S. Army officer arrested in England on suspicion of spying for Russia

British counterterrorism police, working with the FBI and the U.S. military, arrested a 41-year-old American soldier near Gloucester, England, on Tuesday. A U.S. official described him as a field-grade officer, a major to a colonel; he was assigned to NATO’s Allied Rapid Reaction Corps headquarters in Gloucestershire. He has not been named or charged and is held under Britain’s National Security Act. FBI Director Kash Patel called the allegations “of the utmost concern”; the Kremlin said Moscow had “absolutely nothing to do with” it.

Europe quarrels over tying its budget to Ukraine aid

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz has told European Union leaders that new aid for Ukraine is an “essential prerequisite” for approving the EU’s next long-term budget, and the leaders of Slovakia, the Czech Republic, Hungary and Bulgaria pushed back; Slovakia’s Robert Fico called it “blackmail” and threatened a veto. Both decisions need every member’s consent. Ukraine faces a gap of about €70 billion next year. (single source: Euronews)

Afield & Afloat

Ratings are rule-of-thumb estimates from the forecast, the rut calendar and the sun, not measurements of animal or fish behavior.

Deer outlook: Good

The rut is at its peak in north Arkansas, and the barometer is falling ahead of Thursday’s cold front, which tends to get deer on their feet. The drawbacks are a warm afternoon near 77 and a breeze building to about 15 miles an hour by evening. Be settled by 6:05 a.m. and hunt until about 8:35, then sit again from about 3:00 p.m. to sunset at 4:57; at the peak of the rut, 10 a.m. to 2 p.m. is realistic too, with bucks cruising.

Today’s tip: only archery is open until Saturday. In this heat, bucks will cruise the shady, thick cover between bedding areas rather than open fields, so hang near a creek crossing or a funnel through timber. The afternoon wind will carry scent farther than this morning’s calm air, so set up with it in your face.

Tomorrow, Thursday (estimate: Fair): dawn near 47 under a cloudy sky that clears by afternoon, a high near 58 and a breeze of 10 to 15 miles an hour; the pressure settles after the front. Watch about 6:06 to 8:36 a.m. and 2:57 to 4:57 p.m., with midday still worth a sit in the rut. Tomorrow’s tip: the first cool morning after a warm spell often gets deer moving, but it is also the last chance to check your rifle’s zero and hang stands quietly before Saturday’s opener; do your work at midday and stay out of the bedding cover.

Fishing outlook: Fair

At the Crowley’s Ridge lakes (Craighead Forest Lake, Lake Frierson), the barometer is falling fast ahead of the front, which usually makes fish fickle, but the warm afternoon helps. Fish late morning through mid-afternoon as the shallows warm, then the last hour before sunset at 4:57.

Today’s tip: the hours just before a front can bring a short feeding burst. Run a spinnerbait or shallow crankbait along the wind-blown bank for bass this afternoon, and keep a minnow under a float near brush for crappie.

Tomorrow, Thursday (estimate: Good): steady pressure and an afternoon near 58, with a moderate breeze. Best from late morning to mid-afternoon, and again before sunset at 4:57. Tomorrow’s tip: after the front, slow down: crappie will hold tight to brush piles in 10 to 15 feet, so drop small minnows or 1/16-ounce jigs straight down, and work a jig slowly along deeper wood for bass.
