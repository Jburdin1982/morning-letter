Summer’s last visit ends today. The morning opens gray and mild, about 56 at six o’clock and 62 by nine, and the thermometer barely stirs after that, holding near 63 or 64 through the afternoon under a lid of cloud. A cold front crosses in the middle of the day, bringing about a one-in-three chance of a passing shower around noon, but no soaking. Behind it the wind freshens toward 13 miles an hour by evening, and the temperature slips to 54 by six and 51 by nine on its way to the middle or upper 30s by Monday’s dawn.

The rest of Thanksgiving week stays seasonably cool. Monday and Tuesday top out near 50 after starts in the upper 30s; Monday’s clouds should break by mid-afternoon, while Tuesday stays gray. Wednesday, the big travel day, is the raw one, chilly and overcast near 41 with only a slight chance of drizzle. Thanksgiving Day itself looks bright and sunny, about 43 in the morning and 52 by afternoon. Friday begins near 33, cold enough for frost in the low places, and next Saturday warms to the middle 60s.

This is the last Sunday before Thanksgiving, and one with a long memory: it was on Nov. 22, 1963, that President John F. In field and garden: the collards, turnips and mustard still standing in the garden will come into their best this week, for a few cold nights turn some of their starch to sugar and take the bitterness out of the leaves; cut what you need for Thursday’s table on Wednesday afternoon, rinse it well and keep it cool. In the yards, the dark-eyed juncos, the little gray “snowbirds” with white bellies and white tail feathers that flash when they fly, have come down from the north for the winter and feed on the ground beneath the feeders; a scattering of millet or cracked corn on the ground suits them better than a hanging feeder. Out on the farms the combines are nearly all parked, and the stubble fields, with a little water from today’s front in the low corners, will draw ducks and geese to feed as the cold settles in.

Weather lore, not a forecast: an old English fishing rhyme warns, “When the wind is in the north, the skillful fisher goes not forth.” With the breeze turning behind today’s front, the rhyme and the fishing outlook below happen to agree for Monday.

Of Local Interest

It was a quiet weekend at home, the kind that comes before a holiday week, and most of the news is of the neighborly sort.

A ninth birthday spent feeding the “corner people”

Paisli Carroll of Jonesboro turned 9 on Saturday and spent the day cooking for people experiencing homelessness, whom she calls the “corner people,” after noticing them on a street corner while riding with her mother, KAIT reported. She chose the menu herself: chicken and dumplings, green beans, macaroni and cheese and mini cakes. “What’s a birthday without cake?” she said.

Regina McKay, who owns Gina’s Place, a restaurant off South Caraway Road, opened her kitchen to the family and did not charge for the food. Paisli and her parents loaded 12 meals into the car and drove across town handing them out. “They’re our future, these little kids that we’re raising, and we need more of them like that,” Ms. McKay said.

Shop building burns on County Road 456

The Southridge Fire Department, the volunteer department that covers part of rural Craighead County near Jonesboro, fought a fire in a shop building in the 400 block of Craighead County Road 456 on Saturday afternoon. The fire was reported at 12:49 p.m. and was out by about 2:20. A Jonesboro police desk sergeant said no one was hurt and no homes were threatened. No cause had been given.

Habitat for Humanity sets a goal of five more families

Habitat for Humanity of Northeast Arkansas, the chapter of the Christian housing charity that builds homes with volunteer labor and sells them to working families with interest-free mortgages, hopes to help five families into homes by 2027, board president Marie Markland told the Kiwanis Club of Greater Jonesboro this week. Buyers put in “sweat equity,” working on their own houses and others. Money from the group’s Super Sunday fundraiser is paying for its next house, which will be built in Greene County, and the chapter is working to tie its Craighead and Greene County efforts together.

From the State

Federal proposal could cut food aid for thousands of Arkansans

A rule proposed by the U.S. Department of Agriculture, which runs the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP, the former food-stamp program), would sharply limit “broad-based categorical eligibility” in 41 states, including Arkansas, the Arkansas Democrat-Gazette reported Saturday. That provision lets households connected to the federal welfare program for needy families qualify for food aid under looser rules than SNAP’s own. The department estimates 6.3 million people nationwide would lose benefits in 2029, the rule’s first full year.

More than 199,000 Arkansans receive SNAP. Arkansas already sets its income limit at 130 percent of the poverty line, about $42,900 a year for a family of four, while most states go to 200 percent, and in 2022 it enrolled only about 59 percent of eligible people, the lowest rate in the nation. The Arkansas Center for Health Improvement, a Little Rock health-policy group, says the rule would undo the federal basis for a 2023 state law that raised the savings limit for applicants from $3,000 to $5,500.

The state Department of Human Services said it is still reviewing the proposal. The Arkansas Hunger Relief Alliance warned that the state’s free school breakfast program could cost more if children drop off SNAP. The state also faces a possible $48 million federal penalty if it does not bring down its 8.81 percent SNAP error rate by Oct. 1, 2027.

Bar-exam defendant was paid about $28,000 in leave on her way out

Nancie Givens, 62, the former director of the Arkansas Supreme Court office that runs the bar exam, the test lawyers must pass to practice, received a leave payout of $28,200 before taxes, about $19,650 after, when she retired in February, the Democrat-Gazette reported Saturday. Givens is charged with 65 felony counts of tampering with public records; investigators allege that scores for 64 people were altered between July 2020 and July 2025. She has pleaded not guilty, and the charges are allegations.

The payout included the $7,500 maximum for unused sick leave. The state’s administrative office said judicial-branch workers generally are not covered by the state law limiting such payouts. Givens has a court hearing Jan. 4 in Pulaski County. Sen. Mark Johnson, a Republican from Ferndale, plans to refile in January a bill to cut off state retirement benefits for employees convicted of crimes involving the public trust; earlier versions died in committee.

Six lanes on I-57 to Cabot as the state’s 2012 highway program wraps up

The Arkansas Department of Transportation on Friday celebrated the finish of two projects that widen Interstate 57 to six lanes from North Little Rock through Jacksonville to Cabot: about 2.5 miles in Jacksonville, at roughly $145 million, with new and rebuilt overpasses, and about 3.5 miles in Cabot, at about $117 million to $120 million. Director Jared Wiley said both came in months ahead of their 2027 deadlines; the contractor was Weaver-Bailey Contractors

The Jacksonville job was the last of 31 projects in the Connecting Arkansas Program, paid for by the half-cent sales tax voters approved in 2012. For readers here, the I-57 corridor runs 123 miles from North Little Rock to Walnut Ridge, the road many readers take to Little Rock.

Casinos win nearly $199 million in summer quarter

Arkansas’ three casinos won $198.96 million from gamblers in July through September, up 8.1 percent from a year earlier, and paid $25.86 million in state casino taxes, the Democrat-Gazette reported from state figures. Southland in West Memphis led with $99.26 million, up 17 percent, followed by Saracen in Pine Bluff, owned by the Quapaw Nation, with $51.13 million and Oaklawn in Hot Springs with $48.57 million. Mobile sports betting through DraftKings, Southland’s partner since March, grew almost fivefold. West Memphis and Crittenden County receive a share of Southland’s taxes, 19.5 percent to the city and 8 percent to the county.

From the Nation

Trump orders federal contractors to clear dealings with Congress through the White House

President Trump signed an executive order late Friday requiring companies that hold federal contracts, and their subcontractors, to consult the administration before answering inquiries from Congress, to withhold communications the White House considers privileged, and to coordinate their responses with the White House and the agencies they work for, according to the Associated Press, CBS and ABC. Companies that break the rules could lose their contracts and be barred from future federal work; the AP says tens of thousands of businesses are covered.

A White House fact sheet said the order protects “executive privileges from congressional overreach” and accused some lawmakers of harassing private companies. Democrats, who take control of both the House and Senate in January, have named federal contracts, including Pentagon money going to companies linked to the Trump family, as a leading subject for investigation. Rep. Robert Garcia of California, the senior Democrat on the House Oversight Committee, called the order a “blatant attack” on Congress, and Rep. Jamie Raskin of Maryland called it “totally unprecedented and blatantly unlawful.” No lawsuit had been filed; fights over executive privilege usually end up in long court battles.

Trump offers to work with Democrats on a 10 percent cap on credit-card interest

In a Fox News interview broadcast Saturday, Mr. Trump said he would work with the incoming Democratic Congress to cap credit-card interest rates at 10 percent, an idea he promised in his 2024 campaign that the Republican Congress never took up. “I would do it. These credit card companies have made a lot,” he said. Sen. Elizabeth Warren of Massachusetts, expected to lead the Senate Banking Committee, replied, “Let’s cap credit card interest rates,” and Republican Sen. Josh Hawley of Missouri, who has a bipartisan bill to do it, urged quick passage. Banks oppose a cap, saying they would cancel cards for riskier customers or cut rewards; the typical card rate is about 22 percent.

Mr. Trump called House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries of New York, his party’s choice for speaker, a “reasonable guy,” named health care and the debt ceiling as other areas for compromise, and said he would rather not veto everything for two years, though some early vetoes might come “just to set the stage.” Congress must pass a spending bill by Dec. 11 to avoid a government shutdown.

Mathematician claims a computer-checked proof of the Riemann Hypothesis

Levent Alpöge, a mathematician on the staff of Anthropic, the San Francisco artificial-intelligence company that makes the Claude chatbot, posted a note Friday saying he believes that, “working with Claude,” he has proved the Riemann Hypothesis, Scientific American and others reported. The hypothesis, posed by the German mathematician Bernhard Riemann in 1859, predicts a deep regularity in how the prime numbers are spread out; it is the most famous unsolved problem in mathematics and one of seven “Millennium Prize” problems carrying a $1 million reward from the Clay Mathematics Institute.

The proof is written in Lean, a computer language in which a program checks every logical step, and runs to about 17 million lines. On Saturday the groups that maintain Lean and its mathematics library said several independent checkers had accepted it and that they have “full confidence” it is correct, and MIT mathematician Andrew Sutherland, who compiled it himself, called it “a human triumph.” Mathematicians caution that a computer check is not the same as human understanding: Kevin Buzzard of Imperial College London said no one yet understands why the theorem is true. The work has not been through journal review, and the Clay Institute has made no ruling.

FBI investigating hack of its national crime database

The FBI is investigating an apparent breach of the National Crime Information Center, the database police across the country use to check for fugitives, missing persons, criminal records and stolen property, a person familiar with the matter told the AP on Saturday; the FBI declined to comment. Mr. Trump said on Fox, “We just heard about it and I will say that it’s not good.” Who was behind it and how much was taken are not known, and the AP said there was no immediate sign of a link to last week’s string of government and corporate computer outages. The White House’s national cyber director, Sean Cairncross, has been pushed out, and Pentagon chief information officer Kirsten Davies was named to replace him, subject to Senate confirmation.

Coastal storm brings rain and flooding to the Northeast

The storm that flooded parts of Florida last week is moving up the East Coast, with heavy rain shifting from Georgia and the Carolinas on Saturday to the Northeast today, including New York, Philadelphia and Boston, according to the Weather Channel and AccuWeather. Strong winds, downed trees and power outages are possible near the coast late today into Monday, along with high-tide flooding from Virginia to New England that Tuesday’s full-moon tides could worsen. Flight delays are possible at Northeast airports at the start of Thanksgiving travel. The National Hurricane Center had not named it as of the latest reports. Melbourne, Fla., has had more than 22 inches of rain this month.

From Abroad

Climate summit’s deal, silent on fossil fuels, goes to a final vote

After running a record 44 hours past its scheduled close, the United Nations climate summit known as COP31, in Antalya, Turkey, reached a final compromise text early Sunday, and its president, Turkish Environment Minister Murat Kurum, called a session for this morning, Turkish time, to approve it. “We believe that we have put forward a text which all the parties can agree upon,” he said. As this letter went to press, no outcome of that session had been reported.

The draft acknowledges for the first time that warming is on course to pass 1.5 degrees Celsius (2.7 degrees Fahrenheit), the limit nations agreed in Paris to try to stay under, and calls for at least tripling money to help poor countries adapt by 2035, though without a dollar target. It does not mention fossil fuels at all, instead “recalling” the 2023 Dubai agreement that called for a transition away from them. The European Union’s negotiators said they “will not endorse” a decision that “does not even mention fossil fuels,” Panama threatened to block it, and Saudi Arabia opposed stronger language. Any deal needs the consent of nearly 200 countries; without it, the talks could be suspended until next year, which has happened only once before, in 2000.

Drones set a Russian cargo ship on fire off Finland

Two drones struck the Sparta II, a Russian-flagged cargo ship that Finnish officials say has links to Russia’s armed forces, in international waters of the Gulf of Finland on Saturday, setting it ablaze, according to Reuters, Al Jazeera and France 24. The ship was bound for Kaliningrad, Russia’s territory on the Baltic between Poland and Lithuania. Its crew, reported as 16, was taken off by another Russian ship, and the burning vessel drifted into Finnish waters, where Finnish, Estonian and Russian boats fought the fire into Sunday.

Finland’s Defense Forces said Ukraine likely carried out the attack; Ukraine has not claimed it. Finnish Prime Minister Petteri Orpo wrote that “war is spilling over into our immediate vicinity” and that Ukraine “has the right to fight back.” Finland closed air and sea traffic in the eastern gulf, and a Russian helicopter crossed more than two miles into Finnish airspace, which the defense minister said may have been tied to the rescue. Officials are watching for an oil spill.

NATO pledges to defend “every inch” of Poland; Russian strike hits Zelensky’s hometown

NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte, the alliance’s top civilian official, visited Warsaw on Saturday after two Russian missiles and a drone came down in southeastern Poland on Friday during Russia’s mass attack on Ukraine, injuring no one. “All allies stand in full solidarity with Poland,” he said, adding that NATO “will defend every inch” of its territory; the alliance’s top commander in Europe is reviewing air defenses for gaps, and NATO ambassadors meet Monday in Brussels. Mr. Trump has said the incursions were “by mistake.”

In Ukraine, a Russian strike late Friday on Kryvyi Rih, President Volodymyr Zelensky’s hometown, killed a 7-year-old boy and a 20-year-old woman and wounded about 60 people, damaging some 25 apartment buildings, a hospital and schools, local officials said. Mr. Zelensky said the United States wants another round of three-way talks with Russia before Christmas, possibly around the Group of 20 summit at Mr. Trump’s Doral resort near Miami in mid-December, but that he is less sure than Washington that Vladimir Putin is ready to compromise. (details of the talks: single source, The Washington Post)

Mexico captures a cartel boss on the FBI’s most-wanted list

Mexican forces captured Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, known as “El Chapo Isidro,” a drug trafficker on the FBI’s Ten Most Wanted list with a $5 million U.S. reward on his head, after a gunfight that began Friday evening in Guasave, in the northwestern state of Sinaloa, the AP and CBS reported. At least 11 people were killed, seven suspected gunmen and four soldiers, and his allies blocked roads with burning vehicles and set businesses on fire. Meza Flores, a former leader of the Beltrán Leyva cartel later allied with a faction of the Sinaloa Cartel, is wanted in the United States on drug-trafficking and weapons charges. Mexico said U.S. intelligence helped; no extradition has been announced, and analysts warn of a struggle among his would-be successors.

Houthis fire at Riyadh again as Saudi-led coalition strikes Yemen’s main port

The Saudi-led coalition said it shot down three ballistic missiles fired by Yemen’s Houthi rebels, the Iran-aligned movement that holds the capital, Sanaa, at the Saudi capital, Riyadh, early Saturday, the third straight day of alerts there, days after a Houthi strike damaged the tower that houses Saudi Arabia’s sovereign wealth fund. The coalition said it had carried out a “special operation” Friday against weapons smuggling at Hodeidah, Yemen’s main Red Sea port, and struck a major Houthi supply depot in Sanaa. The Houthis claimed coalition strikes killed 41 people in a day, which could not be verified, and warned airlines that Saudi airspace is unsafe.

On the Field

Arkansas State football lost 48-3 at Louisiana Tech on Saturday night in Ruston, La., in its first game under interim coach Jalen Ortiz, the associate head coach who took over after Butch Jones was fired Tuesday. The Bulldogs led 28-3 at halftime, having gained 321 yards on just 28 plays in the first half. Tech quarterback Blake Baker threw touchdown passes of 10 yards to Eli Finley, 82 yards to Marcus Calwise Jr. and 61 yards to Rayshun Hurt, and Finley also ran for a score. A-State’s Trey Owens was intercepted at the Tech 9 by Sam Fongang, the Red Wolves lost a fumble, and their only points came on a 45-yard field goal by Clune Van Andel.

The loss drops A-State to 4-7 and ends any hope of a bowl game; Louisiana Tech, 9-2, has won seven straight. The Red Wolves close the season at home against Troy next Saturday, Nov. 28, at Centennial Bank Stadium, with kickoff set for 2 p.m. according to the school’s schedule; the television slot, expected on ESPN+, was to be finalized Sunday. The search for a permanent coach, led by the firm TurnkeyZRG, goes on; athletic director Chris Pezman has said the school wants a coach who can consistently contend for Sun Belt titles. Hugh Freeze, who coached the Red Wolves in 2011, has been reported to be a top candidate, but no hire has been announced.

Arkansas State men’s basketball (3-2) plays three games in three days in Miami this week in an event hosted by Florida International: Southern Utah on Monday, Long Beach State on Tuesday and FIU on Wednesday, all at 2:30 p.m. Central on ESPN+, according to the school. The Red Wolves come in off Wednesday’s 100-69 rout of Arkansas-Pine Bluff, in which Jordan Pyke scored 20 and Delang Muon had his first career double-double, 15 points on six-for-six shooting and 10 rebounds. They return to First National Bank Arena against Rice next Sunday, Nov. 29, at 1 p.m.

Arkansas Razorbacks football was routed 45-3 at Texas on Saturday in Austin. The Longhorns led 31-3 at halftime; quarterback Arch Manning completed all but a handful of his passes for more than 300 yards and two touchdowns and ran for another, and Texas finished with 448 yards and no turnovers. Chris Marshall caught nine passes for 106 yards for Arkansas, Cam Settles ran for 63, and the Hogs turned the ball over twice; freshman quarterback Hank Hendrix made his college debut late. It was the worst loss of first-year coach Ryan Silverfield’s tenure, a week after the 34-6 win over South Carolina, and Silverfield said afterward he was “very, very angry.”

At 4-7 and 2-6 in the SEC, Arkansas cannot reach a bowl. The season ends next Saturday, Nov. 28, at home in Fayetteville against LSU for the Golden Boot trophy; the SEC will choose between an 11 a.m. kickoff on ESPN2 and 11:45 a.m. on the SEC Network after this weekend’s games.

Tennessee Vols football, No. 9 in the College Football Playoff rankings, beat LSU 27-13 on Senior Day at Neyland Stadium. The game was tied 13-13 after three quarters before freshman Faizon Brandon broke it open with touchdown runs of 18 and 55 yards in the fourth; he finished with 112 yards on 12 carries. Quarterback George MacIntyre played through his knee injury, completing 12 of 21 passes for 79 yards and a touchdown to DaSaahn Brame, and the Vols’ defense sacked LSU’s Sam Leavitt eight times and held the Tigers to 43 rushing yards. Linebacker Arion Carter (ankle) and running back DeSean Bishop (leg) left the game hurt.

Tennessee is 9-2 and 6-2 in the SEC and keeps its playoff hopes alive going into next Saturday’s regular-season finale at Vanderbilt; the time had not been confirmed. LSU fell to 6-5 under Lane Kiffin, the former Vols coach, in his first season there. A new playoff ranking comes out this week.

Afield & Afloat

Ratings are rule-of-thumb estimates from the forecast, the rut calendar and the sun, not measurements of animal or fish behavior.

Deer outlook: Fair

The rut is still in its peak window for north Arkansas, and that is about all today has going for it: the dawn is mild, near 56, the sky is overcast, and a front crossing at midday brings about a one-in-three chance of a shower and a wind that builds from about 8 miles an hour in the morning to 15 by evening. Best windows: from about 6:16 a.m. to roughly two hours after sunrise, and from 2:51 p.m. until sunset at 4:51; in the rut, 10 a.m. to 2 p.m. is realistic for bucks cruising between doe groups. This is the last day of modern gun season in Zone 4.

Today’s tip: deer often feed and move ahead of a front, so if you can sit only once, make it the late morning and early afternoon as the front arrives; once the wind rises in the evening, slip to a stand in a sheltered hollow or on the downwind side of a thicket, where deer go to get out of it.

Tomorrow, Monday (estimate: Excellent). The front leaves behind the kind of morning deer hunters wait for: a dawn near 37, some 19 degrees colder than today’s, a barometer rising sharply, and the rut still in full swing. The wind will be about 12 miles an hour in the morning, easing toward 10 by evening. Windows: about 6:17 a.m. to two hours after sunrise, and 2:50 p.m. to sunset at 4:50, with 10 a.m. to 2 p.m. worth a sit.

Tomorrow’s tip: in Zone 4, which includes Craighead County, modern gun is closed tomorrow, so this is a morning for the bow; set up on the downwind side of a travel corridor between bedding cover and a food source, and stay put through midday. Hunters in Zones 3, 6 and 8 still have their rifles through Dec. 6.

Fishing outlook: Fair

A mild afternoon near 64 helps on Craighead Forest Lake and Lake Frierson, but the barometer is falling fast ahead of the front and the sky is dull, which tends to make fish sluggish and scattered. Best times: late morning through mid-afternoon, and the last hour before sunset at 4:51.

Today’s tip: fish often feed in the hours just before a front arrives, so go early rather than late; a spinnerbait or a square-bill crankbait along wind-blown points and riprap is a good way to find bass, and crappie should be holding on brush piles in about 10 to 15 feet of water.

Tomorrow, Monday (estimate: Poor). The cold snap is fresh, with a dawn near 37 and an afternoon only about 50, and fish usually go deep and quiet for a day or two after a front like this. Best times: the warmest hours, about 1 to 3:30 p.m., after the clouds break, plus the last hour before sunset at 4:50.

Tomorrow’s tip: slow everything down: a small jig or a live minnow fished vertically over the deepest brush or along the channel will catch crappie when nothing else will, and catfish on cut bait near the dam are the surest bite on a cold, bright afternoon.

The Week in Review

At home

A coach let go. Arkansas State fired football coach Butch Jones on Tuesday after nearly six seasons, named associate head coach Jalen Ortiz interim coach and hired a search firm to find a successor.

A plan for twenty years. The Jonesboro City Council adopted the city’s new Comprehensive Growth Plan and Master Street Plan, the blueprint for how the city grows and where its roads go over the next two decades.

A record of giving. The one-day Fill the Food Bank drive on Friday raised the equivalent of 442,215 meals for the Food Bank of Northeast Arkansas, well past its goal, after the food bank warned it had less than a month’s food on hand.

Measles nearby. The state reported five probable measles cases in Lawrence County, Arkansas’ first this year, all in unvaccinated people exposed while traveling.

Around the state

An Arkansas router and a national ground stop. Federal officials said a newly installed router at an FAA radio site in El Dorado flooded the air-traffic network and caused the Nov. 13 nationwide halt of flights.

Bills for January. Lawmakers began filing bills for the 2027 session, led by a bipartisan measure to repeal the 2025 law that added a charge to power bills for new generating plants.

Water money and jobs. The governor announced $261 million for 75 water and sewer projects, about $58 million of it in Northeast Arkansas, and the state’s unemployment rate fell to 3.8 percent with a record number of jobs.

A data center in court. A federal judge refused to approve a settlement freeing a planned $6 billion data center near Wrightsville from Pulaski County rules; the hearing resumes Monday.

The nation and the world

Three leaders in Shenzhen. Presidents Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin held their first three-way meeting, in China, and issued a broad declaration that left out Ukraine and Iran.

Markets stumble. A selloff in artificial-intelligence and chip stocks, worsened by a reported federal look at Nvidia’s accounting, pushed the S&P 500 and the Dow into a correction, the S&P’s worst week since April 2025.

Iran at war, Congress divided. The Senate rejected a bid to make the president end the Iran war or seek its approval, 51 to 48, while the White House moved to limit the incoming Democratic Congress’s investigations.

Abroad. One of Russia’s largest air attacks of the war hit Kyiv, and stray missiles landed in Poland; the U.N.’s highest court ruled that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya people; and the Saudi-Houthi war spread to strikes on Riyadh itself.

The sporting week

A week that began with a firing ended with two lopsided losses: Arkansas State fell 48-3 at Louisiana Tech in Jalen Ortiz’s first game and is out of bowl contention, and Arkansas lost 45-3 at Texas, both teams finishing 4-7 with a game to play. Tennessee, at 9-2, kept its playoff hopes alive against LSU, and Red Wolves basketball routed Arkansas-Pine Bluff 100-69.

The week ahead: Monday through Wednesday, A-State basketball in Miami, Southern Utah, Long Beach State and FIU, each at 2:30 p.m. on ESPN+. Saturday, Nov. 28, LSU at Arkansas, 11 or 11:45 a.m.; Troy at Arkansas State, 2 p.m.; Tennessee at Vanderbilt, time TBA. Sunday, Nov. 29, A-State basketball hosts Rice at 1 p.m.

It was a week of reckonings: a coach dismissed and a season gone sour, a market that ran too far too fast, and computer systems that turned out to be more fragile than anyone liked. Yet it ended in Jonesboro with a food bank filled past its goal and a 9-year-old cooking dinner for strangers on her birthday. Thanksgiving is four days off. May the roads be kind to all who travel this week, and may every table have room for one more.
