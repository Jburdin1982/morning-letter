Of Local Interest

Jonesboro bans camping in city parks and parking lots

The Jonesboro City Council voted 10 to 0 on Thursday to adopt Ordinance 26-038, which bars camping in city parks and city parking lots except in areas the city designates for it. The ordinance defines camping as setting up a tent or other shelter, or laying out a sleeping bag or bedding, for overnight use. A first violation carries a fine of at least $100, police and code-enforcement officers may remove violators and their belongings, and the city can issue a special permit for camping during a special event.

City Attorney Carol Duncan said the rule also covers RV camping and described it as a starting point for keeping people in designated camping areas as the city expands its parks.

The council passed it without discussion; two members, John Street and Dr. Anthony Coleman, were absent, and the meeting had been moved from Tuesday to Thursday because of the general election. Reports did not say when the ordinance takes effect or where the designated camping areas will be.

Century-old bank building burns at Trumann

Fire destroyed the old Bank of Trumann building in Trumann, a city of about 7,400 in Poinsett County, on Monday night. Crews were sent out at about 8:07 p.m. and fought the blaze from outside for roughly three hours, using an estimated 150,000 to 200,000 gallons of water to protect neighboring buildings. The building dated from the early 1920s and had been vacant and deteriorating for decades.

Investigators from the Trumann Fire and Police Departments believe human activity started the fire on the second floor but have not said whether it was deliberate or accidental. No deaths were reported, but a cracked exterior wall fell during the fight and bricks struck several firefighters; Fire Chief Revis Kemper called it a close call, saying the wall missed by a foot or two.

Mayor Jay Paul Woods said the owner lives in New York and ignored the city's letters demanding cleanup and security. The building contains asbestos, and Woods put cleanup at about $40,000 on a lot he values near $1,000. City crews have pushed down the unstable walls, and anyone with information is asked to call the Trumann Police Department.

Fatal shooting on Preakness Drive

Jonesboro police are investigating a shooting at a home in the 3400 block of Preakness Drive in south Jonesboro, which happened at about 3:45 p.m. Wednesday. Police identified Kayunte Sanders, 31, as the person who died and also as a suspect; Jasmine Anderson, 27, was shot in the hand and wrist, and Devandre James, 28, is listed as a victim in the report. According to the incident report, a man came to the home with a gun and began beating a victim, and shots followed. Police spokesperson Sally Smith said a person of interest was taken into custody, but no charges have been filed. The investigation is continuing.

Couple sues the city over a steakhouse permit

Michael Shannon Kee and Elaine Kee of Jonesboro have sued the city in Craighead County Circuit Court after the City Council refused them a private-club permit, which allows a restaurant to serve alcohol. The couple planned 141 Prime, an upscale steakhouse at 2740 N. Church St., and applied on August 24; the council denied the application on October 20, and according to the lawsuit members cited the restaurant’s nearness to a school. The suit, filed November 3, calls the denial “arbitrary and capricious,” says the application met every requirement, and says other establishments near schools have been approved. It asks the court to reverse the decision. The city’s response was not included in the reports.

Votes counted, results unofficial

Voters in Black Rock, in Lawrence County, rejected a half-cent city sales tax on Tuesday, with 62 percent against, according to KAIT. Police Chief Bryan Archer said the money would have helped lower water rates and let the town match grants for a tornado siren, a walking trail and a playground. He said the city budget is tight after losing businesses, including its only grocery store, and paying for storm repairs, and that without the new revenue matching grants may be harder to pursue. These totals are unofficial until county election commissions certify them.

Courts and public safety

Anthony Keaton Graves, 35, of Jonesboro, received a 60-year prison term under a plea agreement, Jonesboro Right Now and KAIT reported Friday. Circuit Judge Randy Philhours imposed six consecutive 120-month terms for six counts of possessing or distributing child sexual abuse material, with further counts carrying suspended sentences, and Graves must register as a sex offender.

Police said the case began with an FBI tip to the Jonesboro Police Department’s Internet Crimes Against Children unit, which led to a SWAT search of his home.

Separately, the owner of an Exxon station at 3109 Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. Drive, Mirzali Lakhani, is charged with one felony count of possessing a controlled substance after state regulatory police said they found 62 kratom pills (31 two-pill packages) for sale on November 5. Arkansas lists two kratom compounds as Schedule I controlled substances. Mr. Lakhani cooperated with the search, was released on his own recognizance and is due in Craighead County Circuit Court on January 25. Charges are allegations, and he is presumed innocent.

Pantry short on turkeys; food drive set for Nov. 20

Helping Neighbor Food Pantry in Jonesboro has fewer turkeys than clients this Thanksgiving, so staff hold a drawing each Tuesday and Thursday shift for the few it has. Coordinator Elizabeth Ivener said it has been a difficult year for pantries and the families they serve, with donations trailing demand, less food arriving since cuts to federal commodity programs, and inflation straining households.

The 19th annual Fill the Food Bank drive runs 6 a.m. to 6 p.m. on November 20 at the Walmart on Highland Drive in Jonesboro, and organizers are asking for nonperishable food. Other drop-off sites include Harps at 8107 U.S. 49 N. in Brookland, plus Walmarts in Paragould, Walnut Ridge, Newport, Blytheville and Osceola. Families seeking help can find a nearby pantry on the “find food” map at foodbankofnea.org.

From the State

Governor Sanders presents a $6.97 billion budget

Gov. Sarah Huckabee Sanders, a Republican elected to a second term on Tuesday with about 54 percent of the vote, presented her proposed 2027-28 state budget to lawmakers on Friday. The plan totals $6,969,073,258, an increase of about $311.6 million, or 3.94 percent, over the current year. It adds $70 million for Education Freedom Accounts, the state's school-choice program, which the governor said more than 50,000 students use, and $20 million for early childhood education.

From one-time surplus money (last year's surplus was $655 million), she proposes $50 million for the State Water Plan and $50 million toward a possible state share of SNAP, the federal food-assistance program, and she directs $75 million a year in interest earnings to Medicaid. Sanders said the plan leaves room for another income-tax cut.

The legislature, where Democrats broke the Republican supermajority in the state House on Tuesday, must still pass the budget when it convenes.

Former bar-exam official charged with altering scores

Nancie Givens, the former executive director of the Arkansas Supreme Court’s Office of Professional Programs, which keeps bar-exam records, was arrested Thursday on 65 counts of tampering with a public record, each a Class C felony, Sixth Judicial District Prosecuting Attorney Will Jones announced. Investigators allege she moved points from some test-takers’ scores to others’ on the same exam, lifting some to or above the passing score of 270, and that records were altered or destroyed; a court review found discrepancies in at least eight testing rounds between July 2020 and July 2025.

Ms. Givens resigned in February. Her successor, Robert Brech, found about 11,400 files had been deleted or made inaccessible in January, many of them records required to be kept for 25 years. The Arkansas State Police and FBI have investigated since April.

She pleaded not guilty Wednesday in Little Rock District Court, and on Thursday a panel of the court’s professional-conduct committee suspended her from practicing law. The state’s justices said most people told they passed did pass, and that affected test-takers will be notified once results are confirmed. The charges are allegations.

Issue 3 fails; Northeast Arkansas mostly voted yes

Issue 3, which would have created new economic-development zones, each with its own governing board, failed in uncertified statewide returns. Supporters pitched it as a tool for small, rural counties, and experts told KAIT many voters were unsure what it would do. Forty-one of the state’s 75 counties voted for it, mostly in the Delta and the South, while larger counties such as Pulaski, Washington and Faulkner voted no. Most of Northeast Arkansas supported it; Randolph County was the exception, voting no with 51 percent. County election commissions must still certify the results.

$8.4 million from the Monsanto settlement for 21 water projects

Attorney General Tim Griffin announced Friday that $8.4 million from the state’s settlement with Monsanto will pay for 21 waterway projects. The October 2 settlement resolved claims that PCBs, industrial chemicals made by Monsanto, contaminated the environment, and it restricts the money to water-quality work such as erosion control and habitat restoration. The Arkansas Game and Fish Commission will administer $6.7 million for 17 projects, including $1 million each for the Eleven Point River in Randolph County and Crooked Creek in Baxter County; four other projects share $1.7 million, led by $1 million for Bayou Bartholomew. No timeline was given.

Governor names appointees, including Jonesboro residents

Governor Sanders announced dozens of board appointments Friday. Among them, Andrea Allen of Jonesboro will chair the 2030 Arkansas Complete Count Committee, a panel to encourage participation in the 2030 census, joined by Gina Gomez of Jonesboro; Landon Wilson of Jonesboro joins the Pollution Control and Ecology Commission; and Dr. Todd Higginbotham of Jonesboro joins the State Board of Dental Examiners.

Matt Jones of Blytheville was named to the Board of Dispensing Opticians and Seth Holifield of Blytheville to the Blytheville-Gosnell Regional Airport Authority.

From the Nation

Democrats win both chambers of Congress

Democrats won control of both the U.S. House and Senate in Tuesday's midterm elections, ending the Republican Party's control of all three of the White House, the Senate and the House. By Associated Press counts reported midweek, Democrats led 225 to 137 in the House with 73 races still uncalled, and held 54 Senate seats to the Republicans' 44 with two undecided; one of those, in Mississippi, was razor-close between Democrat Scott Colom and Republican Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith. National Public Radio reports that Rep. Hakeem Jeffries of New York, the House Democratic leader, is poised to become the first Black Speaker.

Democrats also say they will widen investigations into the fatal shootings of Renee Good and Alex Pretti by immigration agents during a federal enforcement surge in Minneapolis last winter. President Trump said on 60 Minutes that he plans Cabinet changes after the losses.

Stephen Miller named White House chief of staff

Mr. Trump has named Stephen Miller, until now his deputy chief of staff for policy and homeland security adviser, as White House chief of staff, replacing Susie Wiles. Ms. Wiles, who managed Mr. Trump's 2024 campaign, was the first woman to hold the post. Mr. Miller has been a driving force behind the administration's immigration policy, including expanded deportations. Before the election Mr. Trump had publicly expressed full confidence in Ms. Wiles; the change was announced after the vote.

Measles cases pass 5,000

The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention reports more than 5,000 confirmed measles cases in the United States, ABC News reported Saturday. Measles is a highly contagious viral illness that vaccination prevents; health officials generally urge families to check that children's MMR shots are up to date.

From Abroad

Iran: strikes, a blockade and talks

The United States and Iran remain in a standoff that began with a US-Israeli war on Iran this year; a ceasefire collapsed in June. President Trump says US forces carried out another round of what he called minor strikes on Iran, and Iranian media reported two missiles hitting Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz, the narrow waterway through which much of the world's oil moves. The US Navy is blockading Iranian shipping, and US Central Command (the military command covering the Middle East) said it is creating a task force to restrict Iran's overland trade as well, and offered a $20 million reward for information on Iran's nuclear program.

At the same time, reports of progress in talks sent oil prices sharply lower this week; Iran says it will not fully reopen the Strait until the blockade is lifted, sanctions are eased and frozen assets are released. No agreement has been announced.

Ukraine: new talks set for Tuesday

Missiles struck Kyiv early Saturday and wounded at least five people, the Associated Press reported, days before negotiators from Russia, Ukraine and the United States are due to meet in Abu Dhabi, the capital of the United Arab Emirates, on Tuesday. Russian President Vladimir Putin confirmed the meeting. Separately, Beijing has confirmed that a summit among Chinese President Xi Jinping, Mr. Trump and Mr. Putin is under discussion, according to The Guardian's briefing; no date has been given.

Bomb threats disrupt European air travel

Bomb threats have targeted flights, airports and media outlets in at least five European countries, Anadolu Agency reported. Authorities have not publicly said who is behind them. Travelers in affected countries should expect delays and extra security checks.

Paraguay's president impeached

Paraguay's Congress impeached President Santiago Peña, and The Guardian reports he was detained minutes later as protesters demanded answers over the suspension of the Ueno bank; another outlet describes his status as house arrest. Reporting so far is thin and the details may change.

Afield & Afloat

Ratings are rule-of-thumb estimates from the forecast, the rut calendar and the sun, not measurements of animal or fish behavior.

Deer outlook: Fair

The rut is only beginning in Northeast Arkansas. The Game and Fish Commission puts the average statewide peak of breeding around mid-November, a little later in the Delta, so bucks are starting to cruise but are not yet chasing hard. Today brings a cool dawn near 46 degrees, a light wind of 4 to 5 mph, heavy cloud and high pressure that climbed about 0.12 inches over the past day, so expect ordinary, steady movement rather than a banner day.

Best windows are first light, around 6:01 a.m. until about 8:30, and the last two hours of the day, 3:01 to 5:01 p.m. (sunrise is 6:31 and sunset 5:01). Tomorrow looks a shade better, with a colder Sunday dawn near 38 degrees.

Today’s tip: this is the youth modern-gun weekend, so put young hunters on a stand overlooking a field edge or a trail between thick cover and food, and favor the evening sit, when it is warmest and easiest on small hands and feet. With so little wind, keep scent in mind and approach the stand slowly and quietly.

Tomorrow, Sunday (estimate: Good): a sharp cool-down brings a dawn near 38 degrees, with light wind and the rut moving into its peak window, so movement should be better than today. Hunt first light at 6:02 a.m. for about two hours, the evening from 3:00 to 5:00 p.m., and do not leave the stand early: 10 a.m. to 2 p.m. is realistic for bucks cruising.

Tomorrow’s tip: it is the last day of the youth hunt, so keep young hunters on the warmest sit, and set up downwind of a trail or a bedding-to-food travel route, since cold, still mornings carry scent a long way.

Fishing outlook: Good

For the Crowley’s Ridge lakes such as Craighead Forest Lake and Lake Frierson, a mild afternoon near 55 degrees, overcast skies and a light ripple on the water add up to a good day. Fish the late morning through mid-afternoon as the shallows warm, and again in the last hour before sunset.

Today’s tip: as the water cools, slow everything down. Work small minnows or tiny jigs around brush piles and docks for crappie, and crawl a spinnerbait or crankbait along wind-touched banks and points for bass. These are general fall pointers; no current lake report was available.

Tomorrow, Sunday (estimate: Good): steady pressure and a warmer afternoon near 62 degrees should have the shallows waking up. Fish late morning through mid-afternoon, with a second window in the last hour before sunset at 5:00 p.m.

Tomorrow’s tip: with the afternoon warming after a cold dawn, look for crappie to move shallow near brush and docks, and try a slow-rolled spinnerbait on the sunny, wind-blown bank for bass.

Postscript

There is a particular quiet that settles over a November Saturday. The leaves are down, the harvest is in, and the light goes early enough that supper feels earned by four o’clock. It is a fine season for a pot of something slow on the stove, for a porch chair with a blanket, and for ringing up the friend you keep meaning to call. The year is nearly done with its work; there is no shame in letting it rest.

Until tomorrow, with good wishes —
