The moon is new today, which means no moonlight at all tonight and the best dark sky of the month for anyone who can get away from the streetlights. The Leonid meteors, bits of an old comet, peak around the 17th and 18th, and a slim crescent will be setting early by then.

In the garden this is garlic-planting time in Northeast Arkansas: push cloves a couple of inches deep, pointed end up, and mulch them with leaves. Spinach, kale and turnips are sweetest after a frost, and any tender potted plants should come inside before Saturday's near-freezing morning.

Along the ridge and in the bottoms the sweetgums are at their deepest red and the pecans are dropping; squirrels are busy and the persimmons, soft now, are for the possums and the patient. White-throated sparrows have arrived to scratch under the feeders, and the first big flocks of wintering ducks are working the rice stubble at dusk. An old country saying holds that a heavy crop of acorns promises a hard winter. It is lore, not a forecast, and the squirrels do not seem to have heard it.

Of Local Interest

Governor names three Jonesboro residents to state boards

Gov. Sarah Huckabee Sanders announced a long list of appointments to state boards and commissions on Friday, and three of those named are from Jonesboro, according to Jonesboro Right Now. Gina Gomez joins the new 2030 Arkansas Complete Count Committee, a panel set up to encourage everyone in the state to take part in the 2030 U.S. Census, since the population count affects federal funding and representation. Landon Wilson was named to the Arkansas Pollution Control and Ecology Commission, the board that makes the state's environmental rules, for a term ending March 29, 2030.

Dr. Todd Higginbotham was appointed to the Arkansas State Board of Dental Examiners, which licenses and disciplines dentists, replacing Cara Jones, for a term ending September 1, 2029. The committee's chair is Andrea Allen; the report does not say where she lives.

Three men sentenced in A-State golf cart theft

Three men accused of stealing a golf cart from Arkansas State University pleaded guilty to misdemeanor theft of property under $1,000 and first-degree criminal mischief and received suspended sentences, KAIT reported Sunday. Caleb Deshaun Quinn, Taris Durell Randolph and Robert Selvy, 23, each received 12 months of suspended imposition of sentence on each count, meaning the sentence is not served if they follow the court's terms. Each must also pay court costs, fines and fees in $75 monthly installments, plus $1,954.28 in restitution.

The cart had been leased to the A-State Alumni Association for the football season. It was reported stolen September 14 and was recovered the next morning. According to the arrest affidavit, campus cameras showed three people taking it near Red Wolf Boulevard before it left campus, and traffic cameras tracked it to a gas station on East Johnson Avenue. They had first been charged with felony theft of a vehicle. (KAIT's dates for the sentencing were inconsistent, so none is given here.)

Osceola father speaks after autistic son removed from courtroom

The father of an autistic man says his son was mistreated when officers removed him from an Osceola district courtroom after a guilty verdict, KAIT reported Sunday. Daniel Thomas Sr. said his son, Daniel Thomas Jr., was found guilty of misdemeanor harassment and faced 30 days. The verdict, the station said, came at a July 22 hearing before District Judge Catherine Dean. After it, the son began loudly begging and moved toward the judge; police called it a safety breach and officers took him out. Video, KAIT said, captured him saying, “I can't breathe.”

The father says police told him the judge felt threatened. Judge Dean has said her social media post about the incident did not name anyone. A therapist who works with autistic people told KAIT, speaking generally, that stress behaviors can be misread as threats. Osceola police declined to comment on camera, saying an investigation is ongoing; the report does not say what happened to the harassment case afterward.

Free chronic wasting disease testing for hunters

The Arkansas Game and Fish Commission offers free testing for chronic wasting disease (CWD), a fatal, contagious brain illness of deer and elk, throughout hunting season, KAIT reported. Hunters can drop samples at sites or participating taxidermists listed at agfc.com or bring them to AGFC regional offices (call ahead). Anyone who sees a sick deer is asked to report it with photos or video. The CDC and the Arkansas Department of Health advise against eating any animal that tests positive or appears sick.

From the State

Sanders proposes a $6.97 billion budget

Gov. Sarah Huckabee Sanders delivered her proposed state budget to lawmakers on Friday: $6.969 billion for the coming year, an increase of $311.6 million, or 3.94 percent. She says she is holding spending growth down to leave room for another round of income-tax cuts once the budget passes. The state ended its last fiscal year with a $655 million surplus, and revenue in the first four months of this year ran more than $150 million above forecast.

The plan includes $150 million in ongoing Medicaid money, including services for people on a waiting list for a disability-support waiver, plus $200 million in one-time surplus funds for what the Governor calls Medicaid sustainability. It sets aside $70 million for Education Freedom Accounts, the school-voucher program created by her LEARNS Act, and $25 million to cover SNAP (food stamp) administrative costs, with $50 million more in one-time money in case the state must share the cost of SNAP benefits. Other items include $20 million for early childhood education and $50 million for the State Water Plan. The budget goes next to the legislature, which meets in January.

What the election changed in Little Rock

Democrats flipped 11 legislative seats on Tuesday, 10 in the House and one in the Senate, but Republicans still hold a 70-30 majority in the House, according to the Arkansas Times. That matters because appropriations bills, which authorize agency spending, need a three-fourths vote in both chambers, so the minority party now has more weight. House Minority Leader Andrew Collins said more leverage does not mean Democrats will block spending bills, though he promised to be vocal against policies they oppose.

From the Nation

Stephen Miller becomes chief of staff

President Trump named Stephen Miller, his longtime adviser and the architect of his immigration crackdown, White House chief of staff on Friday, replacing Susie Wiles, who is moving to the President's political operation. Miller takes over today. The change follows Tuesday's midterms, in which Republicans lost the House and the Senate. Democrats are expected to investigate Miller when they take control in January; Rep. Robert Garcia has already said he will. Trump also said he had offered the press secretary job to Dana White, the president of the UFC.

Two Senate races still undecided

Democrats hold 54 Senate seats and Republicans 44, according to the Associated Press as of Saturday, with Alaska and Mississippi still uncalled. In Mississippi, Democrat Scott Colom led Republican Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith by about 2,500 votes with 97 percent counted; absentee ballots that arrive by Tuesday still count, counties must certify by Friday, and Mississippi has no statewide recount provision. In Alaska, Democrat Mary Peltola led Sen. Dan Sullivan by only 637 votes with 74 percent counted on Thursday, with more ballots due today and tomorrow; if nobody passes 50 percent, ranked-choice counting begins November 18. In the House, Democrats held 254 seats to 162 as of Sunday, with 19 races uncalled (Yahoo News snapshot, Monday).

Impeachment vote on Hegseth expected this week

Rep. Thomas Massie, a Kentucky Republican, says he will force a House vote this week on impeaching Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, and Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries says he will vote yes. Massie's eight articles of impeachment include allegations that Hegseth violated the War Powers Resolution during the Iran war. Hegseth has called them sham articles. The speaker has up to two legislative days to schedule a vote, and the House could instead vote to table the resolution. A simple majority of those voting is enough to impeach; conviction would require two-thirds of the Senate, which is far less likely. Reports differ on the odds: Massie says better than even, while others call it unclear.

From Abroad

Israel: Netanyahu rival asked to form a government

Israel's President Isaac Herzog has asked Gadi Eisenkot, a former army chief of staff and war-cabinet member who leads the Yashar party, to try to form the next government, passing over Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu after October's inconclusive election. No bloc won a majority in the 120-seat Knesset, Israel's parliament. Eisenkot has 28 days to build a coalition, with a possible 14-day extension, and says he wants a “national emergency government.” Reports differ on his backing: one says lawmakers representing 67 seats recommended him, another credits his four-party alliance with 48 seats. If he fails, a new election must be called within 90 days, and Netanyahu would stay on as caretaker prime minister.

Ukraine: peace talks in Abu Dhabi

Ukraine, Russia and the United States are due to hold talks in Abu Dhabi, in the United Arab Emirates, on November 10 and 11, with a focus on security arrangements after the war, according to U.S. envoy Steve Witkoff. France's foreign minister says European countries will be represented, although NPR reported the Kremlin objected. Kyiv says Moscow's demands, including control of all of the Donbas region, show it is not serious about a deal, while President Trump has floated a security arrangement that “might involve all of Europe,” with few details so far.

South Africa: ruling ANC suffers record local defeat

The African National Congress, the party of Nelson Mandela and South Africa's governing party since apartheid ended, won about 31 percent of the vote in last week's local elections, its worst municipal result ever, against nearly 46 percent in 2021. The main opposition Democratic Alliance had about 25 percent. The ANC lost its majority in six of the eight big cities, including Johannesburg, so coalition talks will decide who runs them. The party's secretary-general called the result “catastrophic.”

Japan: strong earthquake injures at least 22

A magnitude 5.7 earthquake (5.5 by the U.S. Geological Survey) struck western Shizuoka Prefecture in central Japan early Sunday, injuring at least 22 people and shattering store windows. No tsunami warning was issued, and the Japan Meteorological Agency said the chance that it is linked to a feared great Nankai Trough earthquake is low.

Afield & Afloat

Ratings are rule-of-thumb estimates from the forecast, the rut calendar and the sun, not measurements of animal or fish behavior.

Deer outlook: Good

The rut is in its peak window, dawn is a cool 40 degrees and the wind is nearly calm, but a warm afternoon near 67 will keep deer bedded in the shade as the day heats. Be in the stand by first light at 6:05 a.m. and hunt about two hours after sunrise (6:33), then take the evening from about 3:00 p.m. to sunset at 4:59. In the peak rut, 10 a.m. to 2 p.m. is realistic too, as bucks cruise for does.

Today's tip: with the breeze so light, scent will hang low and drift unpredictably; set up with a funnel or creek bottom behind you and be extra patient when you climb down.

Tomorrow, Tuesday (estimate: Good): dawn near 42, a mild afternoon near 71 under heavy cloud and light wind. Watch 6:04 to about 8:30 a.m. and 2:58 to 4:58 p.m. Tomorrow's tip: overcast skies often keep deer moving later into the morning, so stay put until ten.

Fishing outlook: Good

At the Crowley's Ridge lakes (Craighead Forest Lake, Lake Frierson) steady pressure and a warm afternoon near 67 make a good day. Fish late morning through mid-afternoon as the shallows warm, then again in the last hour before sunset at 4:59.

Today's tip: with the water cooling, crappie are moving toward brush piles and docks in 8 to 12 feet; small minnows or 1/16-ounce jigs under a float work well, and bass will take slow-rolled spinnerbaits near wood.

Tomorrow, Tuesday (estimate: Good): steady pressure, an overcast sky and a warm afternoon near 71; best late morning to mid-afternoon, with another window before sunset at 4:58. Tomorrow's tip: clouds let bass roam farther from cover, so try a crankbait along the points.

Postscript

The Last Pecan.

Every fall Aunt Delphine counted the pecans in her yard, and every fall the squirrels counted them too, more efficiently. By Thanksgiving she had a tally of nine, which she kept in a coffee can on the porch, guarded like crown jewels.

“They are a gift,” she told the squirrel on the fence post. “I am not going to argue with you.” She argued with him for an hour.

On Christmas Eve the can held ten. She never learned who had added the tenth, and the squirrel, who sat on the post looking innocent, was not talking.

Until tomorrow, with good wishes —
